Six prompting patterns for research
1 / 6Say who it's for
BEFORE
Summarise this paper.
AFTER
Summarise this paper for a PhD student who knows DFT but not machine learning. Five bullets: question, method, data, result, main limitation.
Why it works: audience and format set the depth and shape. Without them you get a generic abstract.
2 / 6Let it ask first
BEFORE
Design an experiment to test whether X affects Y.
AFTER
I want to test whether X affects Y. Before proposing anything, ask me up to 3 questions about my constraints: equipment, time, sample size.
Why it works: it surfaces assumptions you didn't know you were making.
3 / 6Separate evidence from opinion
BEFORE
What does this paper show?
AFTER
List the paper's claims. For each, quote the supporting sentence with its page. Mark anything that is your own inference as MY READING.
Why it works: you can check every claim in seconds.
4 / 6Ask for its uncertainty
BEFORE
Is my analysis correct?
AFTER
Review my analysis. Give a confidence (high / medium / low) for each conclusion, and tell me which one you're least sure about.
Why it works: you know where to look first.
5 / 6Show the output shape
BEFORE
Extract the key information from these papers.
AFTER
One row per paper: title | n | method | metric | result. Write "not stated" if it's missing. Never guess.
Why it works: structured output goes straight into a sheet or a script.
6 / 6Make it argue back
BEFORE
Is my hypothesis good?
AFTER
Argue against my hypothesis as a hostile reviewer would. Then tell me which objection is strongest and what data would answer it.
Why it works: it avoids the agreeable-assistant trap. Don't reveal the answer you're hoping for.

One pattern per click. Ask the room which pattern they use least. All six are in the handout.

Under the hood · one cluster job, start to finish
From "run it on the cluster" to results.
1Add the host once

Settings → Compute → SSH hosts. Uses your ~/.ssh/config and key or agent. Installs nothing there.

2It probes the machine

CPUs, memory, GPUs, CUDA, conda, modules, Apptainer, scratch, Slurm partitions. Saved as notes you can edit.

3Claude proposes a job

A card asks "Run this job on <host>?" You choose how long the approval lasts.

4Script and inputs go to scratch

Submitted with sbatch. No Slurm? A detached process. PBS and LSF aren't supported.

5It runs as you

Outside the sandbox, with your account's access. Survives a dropped connection. Default timeout: 30 min.

6Results come back

Outputs return to the session. Files over ~100 MB stay on the cluster, with their paths recorded.

Modal works the same way, on your bill: 10 jobs at once, a 12 h timeout (23 h max), and closing the app doesn't stop them.

The scratch directory must be on a shared filesystem for Slurm. The concurrent job limit defaults to 100 per host. The host's Details document holds notes Claude reads and updates: environments, data locations, scheduling conventions. When baseline > peak/2 the FWHM is undefined, so σ₀ = −1,
